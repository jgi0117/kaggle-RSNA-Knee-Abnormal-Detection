# Contusion

## Target definition

Contusion represents a traumatic bone or soft-tissue contusion, especially
a bone bruise or traumatic contusional bone-marrow injury.

The key distinction is whether the report describes an independent traumatic
contusion, rather than bone marrow edema that is secondary to another
abnormality.

## Decision order

1. First, determine whether the report explicitly diagnoses a contusion or bone bruise.
2. If only bone marrow edema is described, determine whether it is clearly traumatic.
3. If the edema is explained by fracture, degeneration, cartilage disease, or another
   osseous abnormality, do not infer a separate contusion.
4. If a separate independent contusion or bone bruise is described, classify it as positive.

## Strong positive evidence

Evidence supporting Contusion = 1 includes:

- explicit bone contusion;
- explicit bone bruise;
- contusional bone marrow edema;
- kissing contusions;
- traumatic marrow edema specifically interpreted as a contusion;
- definite muscle contusion when the report explicitly diagnoses traumatic contusion.

## Findings that require caution

Bone marrow edema alone is not sufficient to establish Contusion.

Do not automatically classify as Contusion = 1 for marrow edema associated with:

- fracture;
- insufficiency fracture;
- subchondral fracture;
- osteochondral fracture;
- osteochondral impaction injury;
- stress injury;
- osteoarthritis;
- cartilage loss or chondrosis;
- subchondral degenerative change.

When marrow edema directly surrounds a fracture or another osseous structural
injury, treat the edema primarily as part of that injury unless the report
separately identifies an additional contusion or bone bruise.

## Soft-tissue findings

Do not classify as Contusion = 1 solely for:

- nonspecific soft-tissue edema;
- subcutaneous edema;
- periarticular edema;
- muscle strain;
- ligamentous edema.

A muscle contusion may count when the report specifically describes a traumatic
muscle contusion rather than nonspecific edema or strain.

## Strong negative evidence

Evidence supporting Contusion = 0 includes:

- no bone contusion;
- no bone bruise;
- normal bone marrow signal;
- marrow edema fully explained by fracture or degenerative pathology;
- nonspecific edema without traumatic contusion;
- explicit statement that there is no bone contusion.

## Fracture distinction

Fracture and Contusion are separate targets.

A confirmed fracture with surrounding bone marrow edema does not automatically
mean that Contusion is also positive.

If the report describes one region as a fracture with adjacent edema and a
separate region as a bone bruise or contusion, the separate contusion may
support Contusion = 1.
