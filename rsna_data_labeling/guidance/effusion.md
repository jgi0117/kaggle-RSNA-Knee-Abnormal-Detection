# Effusion

## Target definition
Effusion represents a definite abnormal accumulation of intra-articular knee joint fluid.

## Strong positive evidence
- joint effusion;
- knee effusion;
- intra-articular fluid described as abnormally increased;
- hydrops;
- hemarthrosis when it represents intra-articular fluid accumulation;
- moderate, large, or massive joint effusion;
- effusion emphasized in the Impression or Conclusion.

A definite mild or small effusion may still represent true effusion when the radiologist clearly treats it as an abnormal finding.

## Findings that require caution
Do not classify as positive solely for:
- fluid isolated to a bursa;
- a Baker's cyst;
- soft-tissue edema;
- intermuscular fluid;
- periarticular edema;
- physiologic or insignificant fluid explicitly described as such.

Terms such as trace, minimal, small, or mild should be interpreted in context rather than converted automatically to label 0.

## Strong negative evidence
- no joint effusion;
- no significant effusion when no abnormal joint-fluid accumulation is otherwise described;
- normal joint-fluid volume.

## Anatomical distinction
Suprapatellar joint recess distention can support knee joint effusion. Bursitis, cystic fluid, and extra-articular fluid alone do not establish this target.
