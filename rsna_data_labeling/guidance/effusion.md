# Effusion

## Target definition

Effusion represents an abnormal accumulation of intra-articular knee joint fluid.

The key question is whether the report describes a clinically meaningful joint
effusion rather than only a very small or incidental amount of fluid.

## Decision order

1. Determine whether intra-articular joint fluid or knee effusion is explicitly described.
2. Evaluate the amount and how strongly the radiologist emphasizes the finding.
3. Give greater weight to moderate, large, massive, or otherwise clearly abnormal effusion.
4. Treat trace, minimal, small, or mild effusion cautiously when it is the only evidence.
5. Do not use extra-articular fluid, bursitis, cysts, or soft-tissue edema as evidence of
   joint effusion.

## Strong positive evidence

Evidence supporting Effusion = 1 includes:

- moderate joint effusion;
- large joint effusion;
- massive joint effusion;
- clearly increased intra-articular fluid;
- hydrops;
- hemarthrosis when it represents a meaningful intra-articular fluid collection;
- effusion prominently emphasized in the Impression or Conclusion.

## Findings that require caution

Do not automatically classify Effusion = 1 for:

- trace effusion;
- minimal effusion;
- small effusion;
- mild effusion;
- small nonspecific joint-fluid volume.

These findings may represent only a minor or incidental amount of fluid.
Interpret them in the context of the overall report and how strongly the
radiologist treats the effusion as an abnormality.

## Findings that are not joint effusion

Do not classify as Effusion = 1 solely for:

- suprapatellar bursitis without definite joint effusion;
- Baker's cyst;
- popliteal cyst;
- pes anserine bursitis;
- intermuscular fluid;
- periarticular fluid;
- subcutaneous edema;
- soft-tissue edema.

## Strong negative evidence

Evidence supporting Effusion = 0 includes:

- no joint effusion;
- no significant joint effusion;
- normal joint-fluid volume;
- only trace, minimal, small, or mild fluid without other evidence that it
  represents a meaningful joint effusion.

## Anatomical distinction

Effusion refers to fluid within the knee joint.

Fluid in a bursa, cyst, muscle plane, or other extra-articular soft-tissue
space should not be transferred to the Effusion target.
