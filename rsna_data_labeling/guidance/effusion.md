# Knee Joint Effusion

## Scope and use
Literature-derived guidance only; not derived from competition labels.

## Clinical/radiologic concept
Joint effusion is excess intra-articular fluid causing distension of the synovial joint cavity. On fluid-sensitive MRI, fluid is high signal and can be assessed by joint/recess distension, including the suprapatellar recess.

## MRI/report findings relevant to effusion
- Explicit joint effusion or intra-articular fluid distension.
- Size may be described as trace, small, moderate, large, or massive.
- Hemarthrosis is an effusion containing blood and remains conceptually distinct from uncomplicated fluid.
- Isolated bursal fluid should not automatically be equated with joint effusion.

## Relationship with synovitis
On non-contrast fluid-sensitive MRI, synovium and fluid can both contribute to high-signal joint distension, so research scoring systems often use “effusion-synovitis.” Contrast-enhanced MRI better separates enhancing synovium from nonenhancing fluid.

## Findings that require caution
- Synovial thickening is not itself synonymous with effusion.
- A Baker cyst or isolated bursal collection is anatomically distinct.
- Very small physiologic-appearing fluid should be interpreted according to the radiologist’s wording and confidence.
